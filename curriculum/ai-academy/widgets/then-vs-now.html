Then vs. Now

Charles Babbage proposed the Difference Engine in 1822. It was a mechanical calculating engine made of gears and number wheels.

It had one job: working out tables of numbers. It was not a general-purpose computer like the phone or laptop you use today.

Size

Difference Engine

Babbage never finished the 1822 design. His later Difference Engine No. 2 was built from his plans in London in 1991. It weighs about 5 tons and has about 8,000 parts.

Phone or laptop

A phone is about 200 grams and fits in a pocket. A laptop is about 1 to 2 kilograms. You would need about 25,000 phones to match 5 tons!

Speed and tasks

Difference Engine

Does one kind of job: calculating number tables, step by step. It only moves as fast as a person can turn its handle.

Phone or laptop

Does billions of steps every second. It runs many different programs: games, maps, video calls, music and AI apps.

What is inside

Difference Engine

Metal gears, rods and number wheels that you can watch move.

Phone or laptop

Electronic circuits on a chip, with billions of tiny switches called transistors.

What stayed the same?

Both machines automate calculation using physical parts: gears then, electronic circuits now.

The biggest change: a modern computer can run many different programs, not just one kind of calculation.
