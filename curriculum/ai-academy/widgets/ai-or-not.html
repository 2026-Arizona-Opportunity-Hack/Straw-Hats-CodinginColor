AI Academy - Module 1

AI or Not AI?

Rule of thumb: AI systems recognize patterns, understand language or make predictions. Many learn those patterns from examples.

A machine that is only automatic - it follows the same fixed rule every time - is not AI just because it runs by itself.

Sort each example as AI, Not AI or Depends. Think of one reason before you pick.

1. A basic calculator

Not quite. A basic calculator follows fixed math rules. Being fast is not the same as being AI.

Matches the guide. It follows the same fixed rules every time and never learns from examples.

Fair thinking, but a basic calculator only follows fixed rules. "Depends" works only if you can name a part that learns patterns.

2. A simple light switch

Not quite. A switch just connects or breaks a circuit. It does exactly what you tell it.

Matches the guide. On or off, nothing is learned or predicted.

Fair if you explain it: the switch itself is Not AI, but a smart-home system that controls it might use AI.

3. A basic vending machine

Not quite. Money in, snack out is automation. Automation alone is not evidence of AI.

Matches the guide. It runs by itself, but it only follows fixed steps.

Fair if you explain it: a basic machine is Not AI, but one inside a bigger system that predicts what to restock could use AI.

4. Netflix recommendations

Matches the guide. It learns patterns from what people watch and predicts what you might like.

Not quite. It does more than follow fixed rules: it learns patterns from viewing data to make predictions.

Close, but the guide says AI: the suggestions come from patterns learned from lots of data.

5. A voice assistant

Matches the guide. It recognizes speech and understands language, skills learned from many examples.

Not quite. Turning your voice into words and figuring out what you mean takes pattern recognition, which is AI.

Close, but the guide says AI: understanding spoken language is a classic AI task.

6. A self-driving car

Matches the guide. It recognizes people, signs and cars from camera and sensor data, then predicts what to do.

Not quite. A car cannot drive on fixed rules alone. It must recognize what it sees and predict what happens next.

Close, but the guide says AI: seeing the road and predicting what others will do needs AI.

7. A basic thermostat

Not quite. A basic thermostat follows one rule, like "if it is colder than 68 degrees, turn on the heat."

Matches the guide. It follows a fixed temperature rule and never learns.

Fair thinking, but a basic thermostat only follows a fixed temperature rule, so the guide says Not AI.

8. A learning thermostat

Matches the guide. If it learns your schedule from patterns in when you change the heat, it may use AI.

Not quite. If it really learns your habits from data, that is AI. Only a thermostat that follows a schedule you typed in is Not AI.

Matches the guide. It depends on whether it truly learns patterns or just follows a schedule someone set.
